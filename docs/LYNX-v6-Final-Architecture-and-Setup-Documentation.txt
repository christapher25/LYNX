1. Executive summary
LYNX ranks the roughly 700 most liquid stocks on India’s National Stock Exchange
(NSE) and publishes a short list of trade ideas, each with an entry, a stop-loss and a
target, plus a plain-language explanation. It never places trades. It is built as a final-year
capstone and is to be delivered as a web app and as an installable mobile app on the App Store
and the Play Store.

The project has three ideas that set it apart from a standard “predict the stock” model.

   1. It judges itself on money after costs, not on accuracy. Every training label comes
      from simulating the exact trade that would have been published: next-day open entry, no
        fill if the stock opens locked at its upper circuit, an ATR-based stop and target, delayed
        exits when a stock is locked at its lower circuit, and the real Indian cost stack (STT,
        brokerage, GST, stamp duty, exchange and SEBI fees, and impact cost) at the rates in
      force on that date.
   2. It publishes only what it can defend. A conformal selection gate compares each day’s
      candidates against a year of matured, out-of-sample outcomes and publishes a name only
        if the evidence says it is unlikely to lose after cost. Zero names on a bad day is a correct
        answer. The gate targets a stated share of losing signals (30%) and the system reports
        how well it actually hit that target, by market regime.
   3. It is built to catch its own silent failures. Most quantitative projects fail by leaking the
      future into the past without any error being raised. LYNX attaches an adversarial test to
      every risky component: a hand-built input with a known answer, checked to fail against a
        deliberately broken implementation.

The model has two named parts. PRISM is the representation: a patch transformer pretrained
on price sequences from two markets, plus a two-layer relational graph network over six kinds
of stock-to-stock link, including promoter-group membership, which has no US equivalent.
VANTA is the decision pipeline on top. It is a named pipeline, not a new algorithm: four
components, each built on published methods and each tested and ablated on its own (Section
9.0).

A language layer (a small open LLM, Qwen3-4B, fine-tuned with LoRA) reads announcements
and news and writes structured, verifiable facts to a knowledge graph. It is extractive only. The
LLM never decides anything; it feeds the graph and it narrates decisions that were already
made.

  WHAT IS NEW, AND WHAT IS NOT

  Every component of VANTA has prior art: triple-barrier style trade labelling (López de Prado),
  ListNet ranking (Cao et al.), and conformal selection with false-discovery-rate control (Jin and
  Candès; Barber et al.). The contribution claimed is the combination, applied to NSE rules and
  evaluated net of Indian costs. In the wording used throughout this document, it is a combination
  not reported in the literature we reviewed. LYNX does not claim a new algorithm, and does not
  claim to be first.

Position against the closest prior work found. Kaya and Nguyen (2025) apply standard
conformal prediction sets to US stock selection, with a random train-test split, no transaction

portfolios, with no false-discovery control and no regime weighting. Jin and Candès developed
weighted conformal selection for applications such as drug-candidate screening, not equities.
Regime-weighted conformal selection over cost-aware executable labels on NSE equities was
not found in that search. The search was targeted, not a systematic review, so the claim is
worded “to our knowledge”.

Suggested wording for reports and slides: “VANTA is the decision-layer pipeline of LYNX. It
combines cost-aware executable labelling, volatility-normalised listwise ranking and regime-
weighted conformal selection with false-discovery-rate control. To our knowledge this
combination has not been applied to Indian equity signals, and each component’s contribution is
measured by ablation.”

What is expected to hold, stated in advance. NEL and FCC are the components most likely
to matter. RW-CS is the real research bet and could show only a modest gain over age-only
weights. VN-ListNet is the component most likely to tie or lose to the CatBoost baseline B1.
Each outcome is reported as found.

2. Problem, goals and constraints

2.1 The problem
A retail investor in India faces roughly 700 liquid stocks and a flood of noisy advice. Most
published “AI stock predictors” fail in one of four ways: they report accuracy instead of profit,
they ignore costs and market rules, they leak future information into their tests, or they publish
a call every day whatever the evidence. LYNX is designed against each of these.

2.5 Scope decision flagged for confirmation: horizons
Earlier documents described three horizons: intraday, swing and long-term. VANTA v1 simulates
trades on daily bars and covers horizons of 5, 20 and 60 sessions. Intraday trades need path
simulation on 5-minute bars, which is deferred. This is consistent with the descope order in
Section 15.4, where intraday is the first item to go, but it is a change from the earlier product
description and should be confirmed by the team and the supervisor.

                                                                     The nine layers and the single decision path

Layers 1, 3 and 4 are new relative to SignalForge. Everything above Layer 5 supplies features.
Everything below Layer 6 narrates or delivers. Nothing outside Layers 5 and 6 can add, remove
or reorder a published signal.

4.2 The decision path, end to end
Data and features (L0, L2) feed the encoder and graph (L5). Text becomes graph facts through
the extraction layer (L3, L4), and those facts return as features and graph edges. The encoder,
pretrained offline (L1), and the regime posterior feed the VANTA ranker. The ranker’s scores are
the only input to the gate (L6). The gate decides what is published. The explanation layer (L7) is
written afterwards, only for published names. Delivery (L8) serves the result, and the self-
improvement loop (L9) records what happened and feeds matured outcomes back into the
calibration store.

4.4 What stays deterministic
The original SignalForge scorer (a weighted score over four rule layers) is kept as an auditable,
cheap fallback and as a feature provider. One change was needed. Its rules were absolute, for
example “RSI below 35 adds 20 points”, which fires for every stock in a market-wide sell-off. Its

outputs are therefore cross-sectionally z-scored within each date before they enter the
feature matrix. A side benefit is robustness to level shifts: when peak-margin rules changed in
2021, every stock’s delivery percentage moved together, and a per-date normalised feature saw
no discontinuity.

  THE TWO-PART DEFENCE AGAINST NON-STATIONARITY

  Cross-sectional normalisation removes level breaks. Rolling training windows remove
  relationship breaks. Neither alone is enough. Together they answer Indian market structure
  changes such as demonetisation, peak margin, mutual-fund recategorisation, T+1 settlement and
  F&O curbs.

5.2 What was measured, not assumed
These facts come from probing NSE’s live archive during Stage 3. They set the effective window
of the whole project.

Dataset                                              First available                           Coverage

Consequences:

        Walk-forward depth. Prices support up to roughly 25 yearly test folds under the 4-year
        train, 1-year validate, 1-year test scheme. Features that need later data, such as delivery

     and VIX-based regime inputs, cover fewer folds.
     Delivery is a per-fold optional feature. Folds testing before September 2019 mask it
     with a missingness indicator. The difference between delivery-enabled and delivery-
     masked folds becomes ablation evidence.
     Universe growth is real. File size grows about 37 times from 1994 to 2024 because NSE
     listed a handful of securities then and thousands now. Early folds have a smaller
     investable universe. This is reported, not corrected.
     Deep history is an evaluation benefit, not a training benefit. 1995 data trains the
     1999 model and never touches the 2024 model.

5.3 Format and parser rules
     July 2024 cutover. NSE circular 62424 replaced the legacy 14-column bhavcopy with the
     34-column CM-UDiFF format from 8 July 2024, and the column names are entirely
     different. Ingestion branches on this date. Equity rows in the new file are filtered by
     instrument type.
      sec_bhavdata_full has one stable format across the cutover and is the preferred
     source from 30 September 2019, because it gives price and delivery together.
     Quirks that must be handled: every column name except the first has a leading space; a
     literal - means missing, not zero; dates are 03-Jun-2024 and are parsed with an explicit
     format.
     Never zero-fill a missing feature. A stock with zero delivery is a different fact from a
     stock with no delivery data. Missing becomes NaN and carries a missingness indicator. A
     zero-filled RSI would read as “maximally oversold”, which is a lie.
     404 is a normal answer. Weekends, holidays and dates before a dataset starts all return
     404. Ingestion treats it as “no trading day”.
     Politeness. A browser-like User-Agent is required. Requests are sequential with a delay
     of at least 0.4 to 0.5 seconds. Retries use exponential backoff for intermittent DNS
     failures.

5.4 Storage and ingestion design

 data/raw/
   bhavcopy/        one Parquet file per trading day (price)
   delivery/        one Parquet file per trading day (sec_bhavdata_full)
   fno_bhavcopy/    F&O files
   announcements/   NSE and BSE announcements
 data/processed/
   corporate_actions.parquet, pit_features.parquet, *_checkpoint.json

Parquet rather than CSV, because training reads thousands of these files repeatedly. One file per
day, because it makes ingestion resumable, makes point-in-time reads trivial, and limits a
corrupted download to one day. All writes are atomic: data is written to a temporary file and
renamed, so an interrupted run can never leave a truncated file that reads as valid. A manifest
records, for every date, whether it was fetched, was a holiday, failed or was empty, so a question
like “why does 2011 look odd” has an answer months later. Re-running ingestion skips
completed dates.

5.5 Corporate actions, universe and point-in-time rules
      Corporate action engine (Stage 4). Prices are adjusted for splits, bonuses and similar
      events. The check: no adjusted daily return exceeds its price band without a matching
      action record, across the full history. Lock detection always uses raw prices against the
      band, and returns use adjusted prices.
      Universe (Stage 5). Per-year membership lists built from historical index constituents.
      Delisted names are retained with terminal values. Walk-forward uses historical
      constituents only, never today’s index projected backwards.
      Point-in-time store (Stage 12). A read as of date t provably returns only information

7. Language and knowledge layer

7.1 Why a structured extractor instead of FinBERT
The case against FinBERT is not language: the corpus is almost entirely English. It is that
FinBERT was trained on US text, so concepts such as promoter pledging, upper circuit, F&O
ban, PSU/NBFC classification and QIP appear to it as unfamiliar tokens. A single sentiment
number in [-1, +1] also discards the event type, the entity, the direction, the magnitude and the
horizon, all of which the knowledge graph needs. Recent work finds that small instruction-tuned
LLMs can match or beat FinBERT with little or no task-specific data.

  PRETRAINING LEAKAGE: THE FAILURE UNIQUE TO THIS DESIGN

  The pretraining corpus must be split by time, not by ticker. An encoder pretrained on
  sequences running to 2026 and then fine-tuned on a fold that tests 2015 has seen the future of
  that fold, and every result would be silently invalid. For a fold testing year Y, both pretraining
  and fine-tuning use data ending before Y minus the embargo. In practice this means one encoder
  per fold. Cutting this corner is the most damaging shortcut available in the architecture.

8.1.1 WFP: warm-started walk-forward pretraining

The problem. Training every fold’s encoder from scratch costs 12 to 48 hours on a T4. Over up
to 25 price folds that is several hundred GPU-hours, several times the free Kaggle allowance of
about 30 GPU-hours a week. As specified in v5, the plan did not fit its own compute budget.

The change. The first fold is pretrained from scratch. Each later fold k starts from fold k−1’s
checkpoint and continues pretraining on the sequences added since, up to fold k’s cutoff (test
start minus embargo), with a short replay sample of older sequences so earlier eras are not

forgotten. Fold k−1’s checkpoint only ever saw data ending before fold k−1’s cutoff, which is
earlier than fold k’s, so the chain never sees the future of any fold.

The model is a relational graph attention network with two layers. Two only: with 700

that the label design removes. Attention is used instead of fixed weights because the right
weight on a promoter-group edge depends on state: group members track each other tightly

encodes membership. Attention encodes propagation, whose strength varies.

8.3 Regime model
A 3-state Gaussian hidden Markov model over India VIX, FII net flow, Nifty 500 breadth and
USDINR change describes the market regime. It is fitted on training data only.

  THE BUG THAT INVALIDATES REGIME RESULTS: SMOOTHED STATES

  At inference the model must use the forward algorithm, which gives the filtered state
  probability using observations up to time t. Viterbi decoding and smoothed posteriors condition
  on the whole sequence, including the future. A backtest built on them looks spectacular and does
  not survive a week live. The common library trap: hmmlearn ’s predict_proba runs forward-
  backward and returns smoothed posteriors. It is forbidden here, and the forward recursion is
  implemented explicitly.

In v5 the regime model no longer routes to hard-selected heads. Its filtered posterior is used as
a set of soft weights in VN-ListNet (Section 9.2) and as part of the RW-CS calibration weights
(Section 9.3).

8.4 Why a small supervised head on a large encoder
The pretrained encoder may be large because it learns from a million unlabelled sequences. The
task head that maps it to a score sees about 5,000 effective samples and stays small. This split is
the most defensible design decision in the project, and it is what makes “we trained for three
days” a real claim rather than a boast.

For every (stock i, decision date t), the system simulates the exact trade that would have been

Design choices worth knowing.

        Incumbency is not baked into labels. An early proposal let names already published
        last week skip the entry cost inside the label. That makes the training target depend on
        the model’s own past publications, which do not exist historically and change whenever
        the model changes, so the target would become circular. Labels stay policy-free: every row
        is a full round trip, and cost_entry is stored separately so an incumbent’s net return can
        be computed later. The accounting layer charges the entry leg once per position (invariant
        N12).
        No-fill handling is a training configuration, not a stored value. Default: a penalty
        value. It stays configurable so it can be ablated.
        Lock detection uses only the hit lists. Inferred price bands were measured at 87.8%
        accuracy, which is not good enough, so rows with no hit-list coverage are excluded from
        primary results.
        Scope. Horizons of 5, 20 and 60 sessions on daily bars.

9.2 Part B: VN-ListNet, volatility-normalised listwise ranking
   1. Warm start. A ridge regression or a small MLP on the net labels, on top of encoder
      outputs (or on the raw feature matrix for baselines).
   2. Fine-tune. ListNet (Plackett-Luce) loss over each decision date’s candidate list. The gain
      is y divided by (ATR% times the square root of h), winsorised at the 2nd and 98th
      percentiles of the training fold only.
   3. Why normalise. Top-heavy listwise losses on noisy returns favour high-volatility “lottery”
      stocks. In the synthetic runs, removing the normalisation dropped simulated Sharpe from
        2.42 to 0.23. Ablation A3 tests whether the same effect appears on NSE data.

   4. Soft regime gating. Score = sum over regimes k of the filtered posterior π_t(k) times
      head_k(z). The weights are posterior probabilities, not learned parameters, so the rule
      against learned gating networks still holds in spirit.

9.3 Part C: RW-CS and FCC, the publish gate
The gate follows conformal selection (Jin and Candès 2023a) with non-exchangeable weighting
in the spirit of Barber et al. (2023).

Calibration set at decision date T. Out-of-sample (score, realised net return) pairs for all
scored candidates from the last 52 weeks, not only those that were published, because
restricting to published names introduces selection bias (invariant N8). A row enters only after
its horizon has passed and its outcome is known (invariant N5). For the 60-session horizon the
effective window is shorter, because recent rows have not matured.

Weights.

w_i = exp(-age_i / τ) times exp(-‖π_i - π_T‖₁)

Age is in weeks, π is the 3-state filtered posterior, and the distance is the L1 norm. The test
point carries weight 1. A candidate gets more weight in the calibration if it is recent and if its
market regime resembled today’s. The decay scale τ is chosen per horizon on validation folds
from {13, 26, 52} weeks (default 26), then frozen and recorded in gate_version . It is never
chosen by looking at test folds.

Computation. Sorting the calibration scores once per decision date and keeping a running sum
of the weights of losing rows gives every candidate’s p-value in O((n + m) log n), for n
calibration rows and m candidates. For about 175,000 rows and 700 candidates this takes well
under a second on a CPU, so the gate adds nothing to the GPU budget.

p-value for each test candidate j (null hypothesis: the trade loses after cost, y ≤ 0):

p_j = (1 + sum over i of w_i · 1[y_i ≤ 0 and s_i ≥ s_j]) / (1 + sum of w_i)

This is the clipped-score conformal p-value of Jin and Candès with conservative tie handling and
weights.

Selection.

   1. Apply Benjamini-Hochberg at q = 0.3 across all candidates for date T.
   2. Keep at most 10 names, in ascending p-value order, ties broken by score.
   3. Zero published names is a valid outcome.

What the gate promises, stated precisely.

      Target. On average, at most q of published signals lose after cost.
      Guarantee status. Exact false-discovery-rate control needs exchangeability. Three things
      break it here: financial time series are not exchangeable; the weights depend on observed
      data (π_T), which takes the method outside the fixed-weight bound of Barber et al.; and
      plain BH on weighted conformal p-values is not covered by Jin and Candès’s result (their
      weighted-selection paper adds a pruning step for this reason). The cap at 10 also keeps a
      subset of the BH set, which the BH guarantee does not cover.
      Therefore the promise is an engineering target. It is validated empirically and reported
      by regime. It is not a theorem, and the project never describes it as one.

Measurement. FDR is the mean of the weekly false-discovery proportion, with weeks that
publish nothing counted as 0. It is reported next to the pooled loss share (total losers
divided by total published). The two can differ a lot when weekly publish counts vary.
Fallback. The Kish effective sample size, (Σw)² / Σw², is logged for every decision. If
regime weighting shrinks it below a threshold (proposed: 200), the gate falls back to age-
only weights and logs that it did.

9.4 Part D: trade levels
Labels simulate stops and targets at k_s and k_t times ATR. If the published levels came from a
different source, the labels would not describe the published trade. So the published levels are
the same ATR multiples: entry at next open, stop at entry minus k_s·ATR, target at entry plus

“expected range” under ablation A11. If they are kept, the quantile-crossing check (a lower
quantile never above a higher one) still applies to them.

The window is rolling, not expanding: four years to train, one to validate, one to test, rolled by
one year. Rolling matters because Indian market structure has broken repeatedly. Under an

contamination, not data.

      Embargo and purging. The embargo is at least the label horizon, per horizon (5, 20 or
      60 sessions), and is a configuration parameter, never a constant. Training rows whose

      dropped. In multi-horizon training the longest horizon governs, which is a real cost of

      Seeds. Ten, not five. Seed variance at this sample size is large. All figures are mean and

11.4 Era analysis and expected results, stated in advance
Rank IC is reported by era across the whole walk-forward, covering the stress windows of 2000,
2008, 2013, 2016 (demonetisation), 2018 (IL&FS and the NBFC crisis), 2020 (COVID) and 2021
(retail boom). Reporting through demonetisation and the NBFC crisis is a harder test than any
US equivalent. If early-period IC differs from recent-period IC, the project has empirically shown
microstructure non-stationarity in Indian equities, which turns a data limitation into a finding.

    NUMBERS TO EXPECT, STATED BEFORE TRAINING

    A rank IC of 0.03 to 0.05 for swing horizons is genuinely good. Longer horizons may run slightly
    higher. An IC above 0.10 is a bug, and the response is to open the invariant tests, not to
    celebrate. A model that reports 85% directional accuracy has leakage, which is a near-certainty
    to diagnose, not a possibility to weigh. Stating this in advance is a form of pre-registration: it
    makes the eventual result more credible whatever it is.

11.5 Milestone M1: the first real-data test of the new layer
Purpose. Test the label simulator (Part A) and the gate (Part C) on real NSE data before any
new model is trained. The scores are 12-1 momentum, recomputed weekly, which is already
validated (the Stage 7 GO used monthly rebalancing). Any failure at M1 therefore belongs to A
or C, not to a model.

Setup. Horizons 5 and 20. k_s in {1.0, 1.5, 2.0} and k_t in {1.5, 2.0, 3.0} are chosen on the first
three years of the window only, then frozen. The window starts at the first price-band hit list on
disk. Two gate arms: age-only weights (needs no HMM) and age times regime weights (needs
the filtered HMM and covers only the later part of the window). Comparisons: ungated weekly
top 10 and the v4 per-stock gate.

12. Invariants and the silent-failure
catalogue

12.1 Rules that are never broken
      The LLM never emits a decision. PRISM and VANTA decide; the LLM narrates.
      No trade execution. Signalling and information only.
      Every scheduled job is wrapped so that one failure never stops the scheduler.
      All secrets live in .env and are read through config.py . One database path and one model
      path, fixed there on day one.
      Walk-forward uses historical constituents only.
      Every signal stores its full feature snapshot as JSON, and every signal shown to a user
      carries a disclaimer.
      Agents write to the knowledge graph and never to the signals table. Graph facts are
      extractive and carry a source_span .
      Pretraining respects the fold boundary, including every warm-start ancestor checkpoint.
      Embargo is at least the label horizon, per horizon.
      NLP inference and training are never co-resident on the 4 GB card.
      No smoothed or Viterbi regime states anywhere. No learned gating network. No
      architecture search.
      No unmatured outcomes in the calibration store, and no mixing of model versions inside
      one calibration set.
      No incumbency logic inside training labels.
      The gate’s q is never described as a guarantee.
      No zero-filling of missing features.

13.2 API, web and mobile
       One API, two clients. FastAPI serves Lite, Pro and Max endpoint families. The Next.js
       web app and the React Native (Expo) mobile app are two clients of the same API, not two
       systems. Neither client holds decision logic.
       Tiers are enforced on the server. A Lite token calling a Pro endpoint gets a 403 with no
       body.

       Charts. TradingView Lightweight Charts (Apache-2.0) in the web client.
       Authentication. Passkeys (WebAuthn) with Argon2id for any stored secret. On mobile,
       Face ID or the Android biometric prompt unlocks a device-held key locally. No biometric
       data is ever sent to or stored on the server. Payments use Razorpay with UPI Autopay for
       recurring INR, in test mode until the registration question is resolved.
       Immutable signal log. Every published signal is written once and never edited. The
       public paper-trading record is generated from this log.

14.4 Continuous integration
A GitHub Actions workflow runs on every push and pull request to the main branch. It installs
the base requirements, runs the invariant suite, and then fails loudly if no invariant tests
were collected. A green run with zero tests would look like safety without providing any, so “no
tests ran” is treated as a failure.

14.5 Working conventions
     Loguru for logging, never print() .
     Tenacity for retries on any network or database call.
     Checkpoint and resume for every batch pipeline over the full universe: progress is written
     to data/processed/*_checkpoint.json after each symbol, so an interrupted run resumes.
     Pure computation stays free of database and network dependencies where practical, so it
     is unit-testable.
     config.py is the only place a path, URL or credential is read. A new module that needs one
     adds it there.
     Every risky component gets an invariant test written before the implementation is
     trusted.
     Long crawls run detached in the background and write checkpoints, because processes
     can be reaped under memory pressure.

14.6 Working with coding agents
Multiple AI tools may write the backend and the clients. Three habits keep this safe.

      CLAUDE.md is binding. It loads automatically in every session and carries the forbidden
     list. A request that conflicts with it is flagged, not silently followed.
     One session per repository at a time. Concurrent sessions in one repository caused a
     bulk file deletion and reaped background jobs earlier in the build. Sessions that must run
     in parallel work in separate branches or worktrees.
     Show diffs before commits, and keep the architecture document and CLAUDE.md in step
     with each other. When they disagree, the canonical document wins and the other is stale.

15.3 Critical path
Stages 1 to 25 are the decision path and constitute a complete dissertation without the product.
The path to the first real evidence is: finish Stage 10, build Stages 11 and 12, then Stage 13 and
the VANTA stages 13A (NEL), 13B (filtered HMM) and 13C (RW-CS with FCC), then M1. M1 is
the earliest point at which the new decision layer meets real NSE data, and it uses momentum
scores, so it needs no new model.

15.4 Descope order
If time compresses, cut in this order. Everything above the line remains a complete and
defensible dissertation.

     1. Intraday horizon (already outside VANTA v1; marked future work).
     2. GDELT historical news, falling back to announcements only, which carry most of the signal
        anyway.
     3. Knowledge graph, keeping LLM extraction as flat features.
     4. Multi-agent orchestration, falling back to a single extraction agent.

The line: Stages 1 to 25 alone are a complete dissertation. Below it the science itself is being
cut. The mobile app is no longer droppable; it is a supervisor-mandated deliverable.

THE FAILURE MODE TO ACTUALLY EXPECT

Not running out of time. Spending week 20 tuning an agent prompt while the cost model is
unwritten. The decision path is the critical path; the agent and knowledge layers make it
presentable. In that order.

Appendix C. Glossary
Term                                                              Meaning

ATR                                                               Average true range: a measure of a stock’s typical
                                                                  daily price movement. Stops and targets are set as
                                                                  multiples of it.

Bhavcopy                                                          NSE’s end-of-day price and volume file

BH                                                                Benjamini-Hochberg: a procedure that selects
                                                                  hypotheses while controlling the false-discovery rate

Circuit limit                                                     The daily price band within which a stock may trade. A
                                                                  stock at its upper circuit can have buyers but no
                                                                  sellers.

Conformal prediction                                              A method that turns past errors into calibrated
                                                                  statements about new predictions

Delivery %                                                        Share of traded volume that is actually delivered rather
                                                                  than squared off within the day

Embargo and purging                                               Removing training rows near the test window, so that
                                                                  labels cannot overlap it

FCC                                                               Full-candidate calibration: the rule that the gate
                                                                  calibrates on every scored, matured candidate, not
                                                                  only published ones

FDR                                                               False-discovery rate: the expected share of selections
                                                                  that are wrong. Here, the share of published signals
                                                                  that lose after cost.

Filtered and smoothed posterior                                   Filtered uses data up to now. Smoothed also uses the
                                                                  future and is forbidden in LYNX.

Kish effective sample size                                        (Σw)² / Σw²: how many equally weighted samples a
                                                                  weighted set is worth

ListNet                                                           A ranking loss defined over a whole ranked list

Net executable label (NEL)                                        The return of a simulated, publishable trade after all
                                                                  costs

PIT                                                               Point-in-time: data restricted to what was knowable on
                                                                  a given date

R-GAT                                                             Relational graph attention network

RW-CS                                                             Regime-weighted conformal selection: the VANTA gate,
                                                                  with calibration weights from recency and the filtered
                                                                  regime posterior

Rank IC                                                           Rank correlation between predicted and realised cross-
                                                                  sectional returns

SSL                                                               Self-supervised learning: learning from the data itself
                                                                  without labels

STT                                                               Securities Transaction Tax

VN-ListNet                                                        ListNet with a volatility-normalised gain and soft
                                                                  regime gating: the VANTA ranker

WFP                                                               Warm-started walk-forward pretraining: each fold’s

Walk-forward                                                      Evaluation that trains on the past and tests on the next

How each component relates to prior art. NEL extends triple-barrier labelling with Indian
circuit-lock rules, stock-specific costs and an executable-trade definition. VN-ListNet applies
ListNet and Plackett-Luce with a volatility-normalised gain. RW-CS applies conformal selection
with time- and regime-weighted calibration in the spirit of Barber et al. FCC is standard
conformal practice made into a tested rule. WFP applies continued pretraining to a leak-free
walk-forward chain. The claim is limited to the combination, evaluated on NSE equities.
