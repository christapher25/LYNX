FINAL-YEAR CAPSTONE · ARCHITECTURE PLAN V4 · CANONICAL

 LYNX Technical Architecture Plan

 Multi-horizon equity signal intelligence for NSE, with web and mobile delivery

This document supersedes v3 and consolidates it with the v4 addendum. It specifies PRISM — a
Pretrained Relational Index-neutral Signal Model — together with the nine-layer system that surrounds
it, the mobile delivery architecture added following the Phase I interim presentation, and the extended
invariant-test methodology that now covers every risky stage of the pipeline, not only the first eight.

The central change from v2 is a shift in where model capacity comes from. Rather than scaling a
supervised model against a small labelled dataset, PRISM acquires its representation through
self-supervised pretraining across two markets, then fine-tunes a small task head on the Indian ranking
problem. Around that core sit an agentic extraction layer that writes verifiable facts to a knowledge
graph, a conformal calibration stage that lets the system abstain, and quantile heads that convert a
ranking into entry, target, and stop-loss levels across three trading horizons. Delivery now spans a web
client and a React Native mobile client published to both the App Store and the Play Store.

## What Changed in v3

Version 2 inherited SignalForge's decision layer intact — a deterministic score combined with an
XGBoost binary classifier, thresholded into BUY/SELL/HOLD. That core is sound engineering and it works
in production. It is also the part that must change if LYNX is to be a distinct system rather than a
port with additions.

### The five upgrades

| # | CHANGE | REPLACES | WHY IT MATTERS |
|—|—|—|—|
| 1 | Cross-market self-supervised pretraining *(headline)* | Training the encoder from scratch on Indian data alone | The labelled Indian ranking task has roughly 5,000 effective samples. Unlabelled price sequences across two markets number in the millions. Pretraining converts the second into representation capacity for the first — and it is the one thing that genuinely rewards days of GPU time. |
| 2 | Multi-horizon multi-task heads | A single 3-day binary label | One trunk, four supervision signals per sample (1d, 5d, 20d, 60d). Roughly quadruples label efficiency at fixed data — and the product needs three horizons anyway. |
| 3 | Listwise ranking objective | P(rise > 2% in 3d) classification with threshold rules | The product consumes an ordering of ten names. A classifier optimises probability calibration for stocks nobody sees, and its base rate swings with the market — so it learns timing, not selection. |
| 4 | Conformal abstention + quantile levels | Fixed thresholds (composite > 65 AND prob ≥ 0.72) | Threshold rules emit a call every day regardless of conditions. Conformal calibration lets LYNX publish nothing when its confidence bound does not clear transaction costs — and quantile heads supply the entry, target, and stop-loss the product requires. |
| 5 | Agentic extraction into a knowledge graph *(from v2 §6)* | Per-ticker sentiment scalar | Retained from the transfer document and strengthened with a verifiability rule (§5.4). Captures supplier-to-OEM and promoter-group propagation that flat features cannot express. |

### What v2 got right and v3 keeps unchanged

The transfer document's operational content is better than its algorithmic content, and almost all of
it survives verbatim:

- **The six lessons (v2 §5).** Date-type mismatch in validation, sparse sentiment joins,

- **The India-specific issues (v2 §9).** Circuit limits, F&O ban periods, the liquidity cliff

- **The override gates.** News gate, volatility gate, drawdown gate, event-proximity gate. These

learned model reliably catches.
- **The core invariant.** The LLM never emits a decision. Retained and strengthened.
- **The deterministic scorer.** Retained — but cross-sectionally normalised (§6.3) and
demoted from decision-maker to feature provider and auditable fallback.
- **The discipline.** "A validated 62% is a far better capstone result than an unverified
73%." That sentence governs this document too.

### Two corrections to earlier guidance

> **CORRECTION 1 — YFINANCE CANNOT BE THE PRICE BACKBONE**
>
> The transfer document proposes yfinance with a .NS suffix as the primary OHLCV source. This silently
> removes delivery percentage, which exists only in NSE bhavcopy and is LYNX's single strongest
> India-specific feature. yfinance's corporate-action adjustment for Indian tickers is also unreliable,
> which collides directly with the transfer document's own warning about bonus issues. Bhavcopy is the
> backbone; yfinance is a convenience layer and cross-check only.

> **CORRECTION 2 — THE EMBARGO WAS TOO SHORT**
>
> A 2-day embargo against a 3-day forward label leaks: the final training observations carry labels that
> extend into the test window. Embargo must be at least the label horizon — 5 days for the swing label,
> 60 for the long-term label. It is a per-horizon configuration parameter, never a constant.

## The Compute Question

Training time is no longer a constraint — runs may take hours or days. This section is about spending
that honestly, because the naive response makes the system worse rather than better.

### The constraint that compute does not lift

The Indian dataset appears to hold roughly 3.5 million rows: 700 stocks over twenty years. That count is
misleading. On any given trading day, all 700 stocks share one market factor, one foreign-flow factor,
a currency factor, and a volatility state. The cross-section is not 700 independent observations of a
relationship — it is closer to one observation of that relationship, measured 700 times with
correlated noise.

"`
independent unit = the DAY, not the stock-day
 250 days/yr × 20 yr ≈ 5,000 effective samples
"`

This is a property of the data, not of the hardware. A larger supervised model trained for a week on
5,000 effective samples fits them perfectly and generalises no better than one trained for an hour.
Longer runs do not fix it. More layers do not fix it. Architecture search actively worsens it, because a
model free to redesign itself against 5,000 samples will find noise and call it structure.

### The four techniques that do convert compute into accuracy

Compute helps precisely where it buys more information rather than more parameters. Four techniques
qualify, and all four are adopted:

| TECHNIQUE | WHY IT WORKS AT 5,000 SAMPLES | COMPUTE APPETITE |
|—|—|—|
| Self-supervised pretraining | Learns representation from unlabelled sequences, of which there are millions. The labelled task then needs far less data to reach the same representation quality. | Very high — days. This is where the long runs go. |
| Cross-market transfer | Adds a second market's history to the pretraining corpus. Microstructure differs, but momentum, mean-reversion, and volatility clustering are shared. | High — folded into pretraining |
| Multi-task learning | Four horizons predicted jointly means four supervision signals per sample instead of one. The shared trunk is regularised by having to serve all four. | Moderate |
| Ensembling and proper HPO | Seed variance at this sample size is large. Ten seeds instead of one turns a noisy point estimate into a distribution. Nested search prevents tuning on the test fold. | High — embarrassingly parallel |

> **THE RULE THAT FOLLOWS**
>
> Spend compute on the pretraining corpus and on ensembling. Keep the supervised head small. The
> pretrained encoder may be large because it learns from millions of unlabelled sequences. The task head
> that maps it to a ranking sees only 5,000 effective samples and stays correspondingly small. This split
> is the single most defensible design decision in the document, and it is what makes "we trained for
> three days" a real claim rather than a boast.

### Where SignalForge becomes an asset

SignalForge already has a 20-year US equity dataset — roughly 800 tickers, cleaned, adjusted,
survivorship-corrected. As a supervised corpus it is unusable for LYNX: different market, different
microstructure, different regulatory regime.

As an unlabelled pretraining corpus it is directly usable. The pretraining objective is masked
reconstruction of price sequences — it never sees a label, so market-specific label semantics never
enter. What transfers is the shape of financial time series: volatility clustering, mean reversion,
volume-price coupling, gap behaviour.

> **THE NARRATIVE THIS CREATES**
>
> SignalForge's dataset becomes LYNX's pretraining corpus. The first project is not repeated by the
> second — it is consumed by it. That is a genuine engineering arc, and it is a stronger answer to "how
> do these two projects differ" than any list of frameworks.

## PRISM — The Algorithm

Pretrained Relational Index-neutral Signal Model. Six components, of which the first is the one that
makes the rest affordable.

### Problem formulation

For each trading day t and each horizon h in intraday, 5d, 20d, 60d, given the investable
universe U(t), produce a ranking over U(t) by expected sector-neutral forward return, together with
a calibrated interval and trade levels for whichever names survive the abstention filter.

"`
r(i,t,h) = adjusted forward return over horizon h

r~(i,t,h) = r(i,t,h) - mean of r over sector(i) on day t

y(i,t,h) = cross-sectional rank of r~, scaled to [0,1]
"`

Two decisions are embedded in those lines. Rank rather than raw return, because Indian smallcaps have
severe fat tails and a squared-error loss on raw returns lets one +40% observation dominate the
gradient — the model learns to chase lottery tickets. Sector-demeaned, because without it the model
spends its capacity predicting the market factor, and the resulting "alpha" carries a beta near one.

### The six components

| # | COMPONENT | WHAT IT DOES | STATUS |
|—|—|—|—|
| 1 | Cross-market SSL pretraining | Masked patch reconstruction over US + India price sequences. Produces a general financial time-series encoder. | NEW |
| 2 | Multi-horizon task heads | One shared trunk, four ranking heads. Each sample supervises all four. | NEW |
| 3 | Heterogeneous temporal graph | Six edge types including promoter group and KG-derived supply chain. Edge weights vary with market state. | EXTENDED |
| 4 | Regime-specialised heads | Shared trunk, separate output heads per HMM regime state. Hard-routed, not learned gating. | NEW |
| 5 | Conformal abstention | Mondrian-binned split conformal. Publishes only when the lower bound clears round-trip cost. | FROM V1 |
| 6 | Quantile trade levels | Pinball-loss heads at tau = 0.1/0.5/0.9 produce entry, target, and stop-loss. | NEW |

### Component 1 — cross-market self-supervised pretraining

The encoder is trained before it ever sees a ranking label, on a task that requires no labels at all.

> **OBJECTIVE: MASKED PATCH RECONSTRUCTION**
>
> A price sequence is divided into patches of 5 trading days. A random 40% of patches are masked. The
> encoder must reconstruct the masked patches from surrounding context. This is the time-series analogue
> of masked language modelling, and it forces the encoder to learn volatility clustering, mean reversion,
> and volume-price coupling — because those are what make reconstruction possible.
>
"`
L_ssl = MSE( reconstructed_patches , true_patches ) over masked positions only
"`

| SOURCE | SCALE | CONTRIBUTION |
|—|—|—|
| India — NSE bhavcopy | ~1,500 tickers × 20 yr | Target market. Includes names outside the final investable universe — for pretraining, illiquid names are still valid sequences. |
| US — SignalForge dataset | ~800 tickers × 20 yr | Transfer corpus. Different microstructure, shared time-series physics. |
| Indices, both markets | ~40 series × 20 yr | Macro context sequences |

Total: roughly 2,300 tickers × 5,000 days, windowed into overlapping 250-day sequences with
stride 5, yielding on the order of one million training sequences — three orders of magnitude more
than the 5,000 effective supervised samples. This is the number that justifies a larger encoder and a
multi-day run.

**Encoder.** Patch-based transformer, 6 layers, d_model 256, 8 heads — approximately 5 million
parameters. Note the inversion from earlier designs: this is large by the standards of the supervised
task and entirely appropriate for a corpus of a million sequences.

> **PRETRAINING LEAKAGE — THE FAILURE MODE UNIQUE TO THIS DESIGN**
>
> The pretraining corpus must be split by time, not by ticker. If the encoder is pretrained on sequences
> running to 2026 and then fine-tuned on a walk-forward fold testing 2015, the encoder has seen the future
> of that fold. Every fold's evaluation would be silently invalid, and nothing would error.
> The requirement: for a fold testing year Y, both pretraining and fine-tuning use data ending before
> Y minus the embargo. In practice this means pretraining once per fold — expensive, which is exactly
> what the compute budget is for. Cutting this corner is the single most damaging shortcut available in
> this architecture.

### Component 2 — multi-horizon task heads

The pretrained trunk is shared. Four ranking heads sit on top, one per horizon, each a small MLP
producing a score for its own label.

"`
L_task = Σ over h of w(h) × LambdaLoss( scores(·,h) , y(·,h) )

w = intraday 0.2, 5d 0.4, 20d 0.2, 60d 0.2
"`

Two benefits at this sample size. Each training day supplies four supervision signals rather than one,
roughly quadrupling label efficiency. And the shared trunk is regularised by having to serve four
horizons simultaneously — a representation that only works for 5-day returns cannot satisfy the 60-day
head, so overfitting to one horizon is penalised by the others.

The intraday head trains on a different input stream (5-minute bars) and is fine-tuned separately after
the daily heads converge; the trunk is shared but frozen during that stage.

> **LOSS DETAIL**
>
> LambdaLoss with NDCG weighting, trained at @50 and evaluated at @10. A pure @10 objective supplies
> gradient from ten of seven hundred stocks across ~250 training days per fold — too sparse to
> learn from. Training at @50 preserves the top-weighted shape while supplying five times the signal.

### Component 3 — heterogeneous temporal graph

Stocks are nodes. Six edge types, rebuilt monthly from trailing data only.

| EDGE | SOURCE | CONTRIBUTION |
|—|—|—|
| same_sector | NIC / GICS | Baseline economic linkage |
| same_promoter_group | NSE/BSE promoter disclosures — ~35 groups, ~150 entities | The India-specific edge. Tata, Reliance, Adani, Birla, Bajaj, JSW, Vedanta. Group members reprice together on group-level news; the January 2023 Adani episode moved ten tickers on one report. No US analogue. |
| supply_chain | KG-derived — agent extraction from related-party disclosures and news | Propagates supplier results to OEMs. This edge is populated by the agent layer (§6.4), which is what makes the agents contribute measurably rather than decoratively. |
| corr_knn | Top-8 by 120d return correlation, excluding same-sector | Co-movement the taxonomy misses |
| index_comembership | Nifty family membership | Pure flow effect from ETF and index-fund rebalancing |
| co_mention | KG-derived — GDELT co-occurrence in the same article | Emergent, event-driven association not present in any static taxonomy |

Model: relational graph attention (R-GAT), two layers. Two only — with 700 nodes and dense sector
edges, two-hop propagation already reaches most of the graph, and a third layer oversmooths node
representations toward a single global average, which is to say it rediscovers the market factor §3.1
removed.

Attention rather than fixed weights, because the correct weight on a promoter-group edge is
state-dependent: group members track each other tightly during group-level events and loosely
otherwise. This is also the answer to the obvious challenge — "why not add a promoter-group dummy to
the gradient boosting model?" A dummy encodes membership; attention encodes propagation, whose strength
varies.

### Component 4 — regime-specialised heads

A 3-state Gaussian HMM over India VIX, FII net flow, Nifty 500 breadth, and USDINR change produces a
regime state. Rather than concatenating a regime embedding, PRISM routes to regime-specific output heads
over a shared trunk.

> **WHY SHARED TRUNK, SEPARATE HEADS — AND NOT A FULL MIXTURE OF EXPERTS**
>
> A three-expert MoE with learned gating would give each expert roughly 1,700 effective samples. That is
> too thin, and learned gating adds a second thing to overfit. Sharing the trunk means all data trains the
> representation; only the final head — a few thousand parameters — specialises. Routing is hard, by
> HMM state, not learned. This captures the genuine benefit (a risk-off regime rewards different features
> than a risk-on one) without the overfitting exposure.

> **THE BUG THAT INVALIDATES REGIME-SWITCHING RESULTS**
>
> Fit the HMM on training data only, and at inference use the forward algorithm — the filtered state
> probability. Never Viterbi. Viterbi decoding conditions on the entire observation sequence including the
> future. A backtest built on Viterbi-smoothed regime labels looks spectacular and does not survive live
> deployment by a week.

### Component 5 — conformal abstention

Split conformal over a rolling 250-day calibration window immediately preceding test. For names the
ranker places in the top-10, conformity scores are collected from realised net returns; the empirical
quantile at alpha = 0.1 gives a lower bound.

"`
publish stock i ⟺ conformal_lower_bound(i) > round_trip_cost(i)
"`

Mondrian-binned by regime, because interval width in a high-VIX outflow regime differs substantially
from a calm one — a pooled quantile is simultaneously too narrow in the volatile regime and too wide
in the calm one.

The product consequence is that some days publish four names, and some days zero. That is the intended
behaviour. The model knows when it does not know is a stronger and more defensible claim than any
accuracy figure, and unlike an accuracy figure it can be verified with a coverage plot.

> **STATING THE ASSUMPTION HONESTLY**
>
> Conformal coverage guarantees assume exchangeability, which financial time series violate. The correct
> posture is to treat coverage as an empirical claim measured per fold, not a theorem cited in the
> introduction. §8 specifies the plot that constitutes the evidence. An examiner who knows conformal
> prediction will ask about exchangeability — answering before being asked is worth more than the
> component itself.

### Component 6 — quantile heads for trade levels

A ranking score cannot produce a price level. LambdaLoss optimises ordering; it says nothing about
magnitude, and target and stop-loss are magnitude questions. A second head is therefore required.

"`
shared trunk (pretrained encoder → R-GAT → fusion)

 -> ranking head → LambdaLoss → decides WHAT publishes

 -> quantile head → pinball loss → decides WHERE levels sit

 tau = 0.1, 0.5, 0.9
"`

The quantile head is roughly 10k parameters and runs only on names surviving the conformal filter.
Ranking retains the decision; the quantile head only sizes the trade.

> **LEVEL RULE**
>
> **Entry** — Intraday: 09:25 VWAP. Swing/long-term: next-day open. Never the signal-generation
> price — that price is not obtainable by any user.
> **Stop-loss** — min( entry × (1 + q_10) , entry - 1.5 × ATR ) — the tighter
> of the two. The ATR floor is the sanity check: quantile estimates on financial returns are noisy, and a
> bad q_10 gives a stop 8% away that protects nothing.
> **Target** — entry × (1 + q_50), floored at 1.5R. Use the median, not q_90 — a
> level reached one time in ten is decorative.

> **WHAT STOP-LOSSES DO TO THE EVALUATION**
>
> Rank IC measures return at the end of the horizon. A stop-loss is path-dependent: a 5-day trade ending
> +3% that dipped -2% on day two gets stopped out. Realised performance and rank IC therefore diverge,
> and rank IC alone stops being a sufficient report. §8 adds hit rate, realised R, stop-out rate, and
> expected value per signal.
> This also yields a genuinely interesting ablation: does the stop-loss improve or degrade net expected
> value versus holding to horizon? The answer is not obvious. The prior worth testing is that stops help
> intraday, where adverse moves persist, and hurt swing, where mean reversion within the horizon is common.

### News — GDELT as the historical backbone

The transfer document proposed NewsAPI plus RSS. That combination cannot support this project, for a
specific reason: RSS feeds have no history. They deliver what is published now. A walk-forward
evaluation over twenty years needs news from 2010, and no RSS feed provides it. NewsAPI's free tier is
capped at 100 requests per day with historical access limited to roughly one month, and its paid tiers
start around 449/month.

> **GDELT SOLVES THE HISTORICAL NEWS PROBLEM**
>
> GDELT is a free, open dataset monitoring news from every country, covering 100+ countries and dozens of
> languages, with historical data reaching back decades and 15-minute update cycles. It includes tone,
> themes, entity, and geolocation annotations, and its Global Knowledge Graph connects entities and
> events. Access is via BigQuery for bulk historical extraction and via the DOC 2.0 API for recent queries.
> Two caveats worth knowing. GDELT is built for global event monitoring rather than finance, so entity
> resolution to NSE tickers must be built rather than assumed. And the newer GDELT Cloud product has
> sparse coverage before March 2026 — use the core GDELT 2.0 GKG tables via BigQuery for historical
> work, not GDELT Cloud.

| SOURCE | COST | HISTORY | ROLE IN LYNX |
|—|—|—|—|
| GDELT 2.0 GKG (BigQuery) | Free | Deep | Historical backbone. Enables news features across the full walk-forward, plus the co_mention graph edge. |
| NSE / BSE announcements | Free | Deep | Highest signal-to-noise source available. Official, structured, timestamped, ticker-tagged by construction — no entity resolution needed. Results, orders, ratings, promoter actions. |
| RSS — ET, Moneycontrol, Mint, Business Standard | Free | None | Live layer only. Begins accumulating history from day one of the build. |
| NewsAPI | Free | ~1 month | Optional live supplement. Not load-bearing. |

> **CORPORATE ANNOUNCEMENTS DESERVE MORE WEIGHT THAN NEWS**
>
> NSE announcements are already ticker-tagged, timestamped, and categorised. They require no entity
> resolution, carry no editorial noise, and are the actual events that news articles report on — usually
> hours later. For a system whose features must be point-in-time correct, an announcement feed is
> strictly better than a news feed. Build the announcement pipeline before the news pipeline.

## Language Layer

FinBERT is replaced — but for a narrower and better-evidenced reason than previously argued, and the
replacement changes what the layer produces, not just which model produces it.

### Why FinBERT goes

An earlier version of this argument claimed FinBERT fails on Hindi-language sources. That was largely
irrelevant: the actual corpus — Economic Times, Moneycontrol, Mint, Business Standard, NSE
announcements — is overwhelmingly English. The claim was wrong and is withdrawn.

The real case is different and stronger. Recent work fine-tuning lightweight LLMs for financial
sentiment finds that LLMs can surpass FinBERT while using only a minimal proportion of training data, or
none at all, with Qwen3 8B and Llama3 8B performing best across most scenarios in both zero-shot and
few-shot settings. Separately, FinGPT's v3 series — general LLMs LoRA-fine-tuned on news and sentiment
data — reports the best scores on most financial sentiment datasets at low cost, trainable on a single
consumer GPU.

Two further considerations specific to LYNX:

- **Domain concepts, not language.** FinBERT was trained on US financial text. Promoter
pledging, upper circuit, F&O ban, PSU/NBFC classification, and QIP do not appear in its training
distribution. It handles them as unfamiliar tokens rather than as concepts.
- **Scalar sentiment is a lossy output.** A single number in [-1, +1] discards the event type,
the affected entity, the direction, the magnitude, and the horizon — all of which LYNX needs for its
knowledge graph.

### What replaces it — structured event extraction

An instruction-tuned open LLM (Qwen3 8B or Llama3 8B, LoRA-fine-tuned) emits a structured record per
article or announcement rather than a scalar:

> **RECORD SCHEMA**
>
>
> entity: "TATAMOTORS" // resolved to NSE symbol
> event_type: "order_win" // enum, ~18 categories
> polarity: +1 // -1 | 0 | +1
> magnitude: "material" // minor | material | transformative
> horizon: "medium" // immediate | short | medium | long
> confidence: 0.82
> source_span: "..." // verbatim text supporting the extraction
> related: ["TATAPOWER"] // co-mentioned entities → graph edge

Event type enum: results_beat, results_miss, guidance_up, guidance_down, order_win,
capex_announcement, rating_upgrade, rating_downgrade, regulatory_action, promoter_buy,
promoter_sell, pledge_increase, pledge_release, mna, litigation, management_change, qip_fundraise,
other.

Each field becomes a feature. \textttrelated populates the co_mention graph edge.
\textttsource_span is what makes the extraction verifiable — see §5.4.

### Hardware routing

The local GPU is 4 GB, which cannot fine-tune an 8B model. The work splits:

| STAGE | WHERE | NOTE |
|—|—|—|
| SSL pretraining (§3.3) | Cloud GPU — Kaggle T4 (16 GB, free tier) or Colab | The multi-day runs. Checkpoint aggressively; free tiers have session limits. |
| LLM LoRA fine-tune | Cloud GPU | QLoRA on an 8B model needs ~8--12 GB |
| LLM batch inference | Cloud, or API | Nightly over new articles only. Historical backfill is a one-time bulk job. |
| PRISM supervised fine-tune | Local 4 GB | ~500k parameter task head. Fits comfortably. |
| Nightly inference | Local 4 GB | 700 stocks, one forward pass |

> **CARRIED FROM THE TRANSFER DOCUMENT**
>
> NLP inference and model training must never be co-resident on the 4 GB card. Run extraction as a nightly
> stage that completes before training begins. Sequential, always.

### The rule that keeps the agent layer honest

The transfer document's §6 states that agents enrich features but do not vote. That is weaker than it
sounds — a feature entering the model does affect the decision. The rule that makes the design actually
safe:

> **EXTRACTIVE, NOT GENERATIVE**
>
> Facts written to the knowledge graph must be extractive and verifiable, not generative judgments.
> ✓ "Company A supplies Company B" — extracted from a related-party disclosure, with a
> source_span, checkable against the original document.
> × "Sector sentiment is deteriorating" — an LLM opinion with no ground truth and no way to
> audit it.
> Extraction can be validated: hand-label 500 articles, measure precision and recall per event type, and
> report it. Judgment cannot be validated, and a hallucinated judgment entering the feature matrix is
> exactly the failure mode the LLM-never-decides invariant exists to prevent. This rule is what lets the
> agent layer contribute measurably rather than decoratively — and it is a reportable result in its own
> right.

## System Architecture — Nine Layers

Layers 1, 3, and 4 are new relative to SignalForge. Layer 5 is the only component that emits a
decision; everything above it supplies features and everything below it narrates or delivers.

### Layer responsibilities

| L | LAYER | CADENCE | RESPONSIBILITY |
|—|—|—|—|
| 0 | Data | Nightly + bulk | Ingest, validate, adjust for corporate actions, enforce point-in-time correctness |
| 1 | SSL pretraining | Per fold, offline | Masked patch reconstruction over the two-market corpus. Produces the encoder checkpoint. |
| 2 | Features + scorer | Nightly | Price, delivery, F&O, macro blocks. Deterministic scorer retained as feature and fallback. Override gates applied. |
| 3 | Agent extraction | Nightly | LangGraph orchestration over announcements and news. Structured, extractive records only. |
| 4 | Knowledge graph | Nightly write, monthly graph rebuild | Entity-event store. Emits relational features and two graph edge types. |
| 5 | PRISM core | Nightly | The only decision-maker. Four horizon rankings plus quantile levels. |
| 6 | Conformal | Nightly | Calibration, cost-aware abstention, final publish set |
| 7 | Explanation | Nightly, published names only | LLM narration grounded in SHAP attribution and retrieved evidence |
| 8 | Delivery | Continuous | API, clients, tiering, immutable prediction log — web and mobile; see §12 for the mobile delivery architecture |
| 9 | Self-improvement | Weekly | Outcome recording, scheduled retrain, conformal recalibration, feature drift monitoring |

> **THE DECISION PATH, END TO END**
>
> L0 Data (bhavcopy + delivery% · F&O · flows · macro · GDELT · NSE
> announcements · SignalForge US corpus) → L1 SSL pretraining (offline, masked patches, US +
> India) → L2 Features + deterministic scorer (cross-sectionally z-scored, gates applied) → L3
> Agent extraction — LangGraph (structured events, extractive only) → L4 Knowledge graph (relational
> features + graph edges) → L5 PRISM ranking core (pretrained encoder → R-GAT → regime heads
> → 4 horizon rankers + quantile head — the only component that emits a decision) → L6 Conformal

> L7 Explanation (LLM narrates decisions already made, grounded in SHAP + retrieved evidence) → L8
> Delivery (FastAPI, web + mobile, tiered, immutable prediction log) → L9 Self-improvement loop
> (outcome recorder, scheduled retrain, recalibration).
> L1 runs offline per fold. L2--L8 run nightly. L9 runs weekly.

### The deterministic scorer, retained but normalised

SignalForge's four-layer weighted score is kept — it is auditable, cheap, and gives a non-ML fallback
if the model pipeline fails. One change is required.

The original rules are absolute: RSI < 35 → +20 fires for every stock in a market-wide selloff,
which is the same beta contamination §3.1 removes at the label level. The scorer's component outputs
are therefore cross-sectionally z-scored within each date before entering the feature matrix.

This also happens to be a free defence against structural breaks. When peak margin rules cut retail
leverage in 2021, every stock's delivery percentage shifted together — a time-series normalised
feature sees a huge discontinuity, while a cross-sectionally z-scored feature sees nothing, because the
level moved and the ranking did not.

> **THE TWO-PART DEFENCE, STATED ONCE**
>
> Cross-sectional normalisation kills level breaks. Rolling training windows kill relationship breaks.
> Neither alone is sufficient. Together they are a complete and defensible answer to Indian microstructure
> non-stationarity — demonetisation, peak margin, MF recategorisation, T+1, F&O curbs.

## Training Protocol

### Four stages

| # | STAGE | DATA | DURATION | OUTPUT |
|—|—|—|—|—|
| 1 | SSL pretraining | ~1M unlabelled sequences, US + India, ending before the fold boundary | 12--48 h per fold | Encoder checkpoint |
| 2 | Multi-horizon supervised fine-tune | Fold training window, 4 labels per sample | 1--3 h × 10 seeds | Ranking + quantile heads |
| 3 | Regime head specialisation | Same window, partitioned by HMM state | < 1 h | Three output heads |
| 4 | Conformal calibration | Rolling 250-day window before test | Minutes | Per-regime quantiles |

### Walk-forward discipline

Rolling window, not expanding. Four years train, one validate, one test, rolled by one year — roughly
fifteen out-of-sample folds over twenty-one years.

Rolling matters because Indian market structure has broken repeatedly. Under an expanding window the
2025 model is partly fitted on 2006 microstructure, which is contamination rather than data. Under a
rolling window, 2006 trains the 2010 model and never touches the 2025 model.

The benefit of deep history is therefore an evaluation benefit: ten years gives ~5 test folds,
twenty-one gives ~15. That is the difference between "our IC was 0.04" — an anecdote — and
"our IC was 0.04 \pm 0.015 across fifteen out-of-sample years" — a result.

> **PURGING AND EMBARGO**
>
> Embargo equals the label horizon, per horizon: 1 day intraday, 5 days swing, 20 and 60 for the longer
> heads. In multi-task training the longest horizon governs — a 60-day head means a 60-day embargo for
> the whole batch. This is a real cost of multi-task learning and must be stated rather than quietly
> ignored.

> **SEEDS**
>
> Ten seeds, not five. Compute is free and seed variance at 5,000 effective samples is large. All reported
> figures are mean \pm standard deviation across seeds and folds. A single-seed number is noise, and
> quoting one in a report is not recoverable under questioning.

### Where the long runs actually go

| COMPUTE SPENT ON | HELPS? | WHY |
|—|—|—|
| SSL pretraining, 15 folds | Yes | Learns from a million unlabelled sequences. The dominant compute line and the one that earns its cost. |
| 10-seed ensembling | Yes | Turns a noisy point estimate into a distribution. Embarrassingly parallel. |
| Nested hyperparameter search | Yes | Prevents tuning on the test fold — the quiet way most reported results inflate |
| Historical LLM extraction backfill | Yes | One-time bulk job over years of announcements; unlocks news features across the whole walk-forward |
| Larger supervised head | No | 5,000 effective samples. More parameters fit noise. |
| More epochs on the task head | No | Early stopping on validation rank IC governs; extra epochs overfit |
| Neural architecture search | Actively harmful | A model free to redesign itself against 5,000 samples finds noise and reports it as structure |

## Evaluation

For a project of this kind the evaluation chapter carries more weight than the architecture chapter. A
modest model measured honestly is a stronger submission than a sophisticated model measured carelessly,
and any examiner who knows the field sees the difference immediately.

### Metrics

| METRIC | DEFINITION | PURPOSE |
|—|—|—|
| Rank IC | Daily Spearman correlation, predicted vs realised cross-sectional rank | Primary. Measures ordering skill directly. |
| IC-IR | Mean rank IC \div its standard deviation | Consistency. IC 0.03 every day beats IC 0.06 half the time. |
| NDCG@10 | Top-weighted ranking quality at 10 | The product metric — this is the shortlist |
| Net top-decile spread | Top minus bottom decile, after all costs | The economic bottom line |
| Hit rate, realised R, stop-out rate | Path-dependent outcomes with SL active | Required once §3.8 levels exist — rank IC alone no longer suffices |
| Expected value per signal | Net of costs, with SL and target applied | The number that actually determines whether the product is useful |
| Conformal coverage | Realised vs nominal, per fold, per regime bin | The evidence for the calibration claim |
| Extraction precision/recall | Per event type, against 500 hand-labelled articles | The evidence that the agent layer is trustworthy |
| Abstention rate | Share of days publishing fewer than 10 | Characterises behaviour. A healthy system abstains. |

### The Indian transaction cost model

> **BUILD THIS IN STAGE 6 — BEFORE FEATURES, BEFORE ANY MODEL**
>
> The largest risk to this project is not that the architecture fails. It is that a sophisticated system
> is built over twenty weeks and then found to have no edge net of Indian frictions. Build the cost model
> early and run a momentum baseline through it in week four. A negative answer in week four leaves time to
> respond by widening the horizon or tightening the universe. The same answer in week twenty leaves
> nothing.

| COMPONENT | DELIVERY (SWING / LONG-TERM) | INTRADAY |
|—|—|—|
| STT | 0.1% both sides | 0.025% sell side only |
 - Brokerage | \multicolumn2lDiscount-broker schedule; document the assumption
 - Exchange charges | \multicolumn2lNSE published schedule
 - GST | \multicolumn2lOn brokerage + transaction charges — a tax on the fees, easy to omit, and omitting it flatters results
 - Stamp duty | \multicolumn2lBuy side, state-varying
 - SEBI turnover fee | \multicolumn2lSmall but present
 - Impact cost | \multicolumn2lNSE published, per security. The component most projects omit and the one that eats marginal signals. There is no excuse for a flat slippage assumption when NSE publishes the real figure.
| **Round-trip total** | **~0.25--0.30%** | **~0.06--0.10%** |

> **A CORRECTION WORTH CARRYING INTO THE REPORT**
>
> Intraday is cheaper per round trip than delivery in India, not more expensive — STT is 0.025%
> sell-side only versus 0.1% both sides. What makes intraday hard is signal-to-noise and execution
> timing, not cost. An earlier version of this analysis had it backwards.

All rates live in a date-versioned configuration table, so a backtest over 2011 applies 2011's STT rate.
Point-in-time correctness applies to costs as much as to features, and STT rates have changed materially
within the sample.

### The ablation suite

Each ablation isolates exactly one claim. This is what makes the project research rather than a
demonstration.

| # | CONFIGURATION | CLAIM TESTED |
|—|—|—|
| A0 | Momentum 12-1 | The dumb baseline. Many published ML equity results fail to beat it net of costs. |
| A1 | SignalForge port — deterministic score + XGBoost binary, thresholded | The headline comparison. Does the reformulation beat the predecessor on identical Indian data under identical walk-forward conditions? This is the experiment that substantiates the entire v3 rationale. |
| A2 | LightGBM lambdarank, no pretraining, no graph | Isolates the ranking objective from everything else |
| A3 | PRISM - SSL pretraining (random init encoder) | Does cross-market pretraining help? The core claim of §3.3. If yes, this is a publishable transfer-learning result for emerging-market equities. |
| A4 | PRISM - US corpus (India-only pretraining) | Separates "pretraining helps" from "cross-market transfer helps" — two different claims |
| A5 | PRISM - multi-horizon (5d head only) | Does multi-task supervision improve label efficiency? |
| A6 | PRISM - graph | Does relational structure contribute? |
| A7 | PRISM - promoter-group edges only | The India-specific claim. Sector and correlation edges retained. Degradation here demonstrates a market-structure effect with no US analogue. |
| A8 | PRISM - KG features | Does the agent layer contribute measurably, or only decoratively? |
| A9 | PRISM - regime heads | Does regime specialisation beat a regime embedding? |
| A10 | PRISM - conformal (publish top-10 always) | Does abstention improve net EV, or only reduce volume? |
| A11 | PRISM - stop-loss (hold to horizon) | Path-dependence: does the SL add or destroy expected value, per horizon? |
| A12 | PRISM full | The system as specified |

A1, A3, and A7 are the three that matter. A1 substantiates the refinement claim against SignalForge. A3
substantiates the pretraining claim, which is the novel methodological contribution. A7 substantiates
the India-specific claim. A negative result on any of them is still a result and should be reported as
one — a well-measured negative finding outranks an unmeasured positive claim.

### Era analysis and stress windows

Report rank IC by era across the full walk-forward, covering the stress windows the transfer document
correctly identified: 2008 global financial crisis, 2013 taper tantrum, 2016 demonetisation, 2018
IL&FS/NBFC crisis, 2020 COVID, 2021 retail boom.

Reporting performance through demonetisation and the NBFC crisis is a harder test than any US equivalent
and is a strong capstone result in itself. If early-period IC differs materially from recent-period IC,
the project has empirically demonstrated microstructure non-stationarity in Indian equities backed by
fifteen out-of-sample folds — which converts a data limitation into a finding, and answers the
inevitable question "why a four-year training window?" with "because we measured where the useful
history ends."

### Expected results, pre-registered

> **THE NUMBERS TO EXPECT, STATED BEFORE TRAINING**
>
> Rank IC 0.03--0.05 for swing is genuinely good. Long-term may run slightly higher; intraday will be
> lower, perhaps 0.02, because intraday returns carry more microstructure noise.
> IC above 0.10 means there is a bug. Treat it as a bug report and open the invariant tests — it will be
> one of the failures in §11.2. If the model reports 85% directional accuracy, there is leakage; that is
> not a possibility to weigh but a near-certainty to diagnose.
> Stating these figures in advance is a form of pre-registration. It makes the eventual result more
> credible whatever it turns out to be, and it removes the temptation to rationalise an implausibly good
> outcome rather than debug it.

## Invariants and Risk

### Invariants — carried from SignalForge, extended

> **CARRY UNCHANGED**
>
>
> - The LLM never emits a BUY/SELL/HOLD decision. PRISM decides; the LLM narrates.
> - No trade execution. Signalling and information only.
> - Every scheduled job wrapped so one failure never stops the scheduler.
> - All secrets in .env, loaded through config.py. Never hardcoded.
> - Walk-forward on historical constituents only. Never today's index projected backwards.
> - Every signal row stores its full feature snapshot as JSON — required for retraining and audit.
> - Every signal shown to a user carries a disclaimer.
> - One database path, one model path, fixed in config.py on day one.
>

> **NEW IN V3**
>
>
> - Agents write to the knowledge graph. Agents never write to the signals table.
> - KG facts are extractive and carry a source_span. No generative judgments enter the feature
> matrix.
> - Pretraining respects the fold boundary. No encoder is fine-tuned on a fold whose test period
> precedes its pretraining cutoff.
> - Embargo ≥ label horizon, per horizon. Configuration parameter, never a constant.
> - NLP inference and model training are never co-resident on the 4 GB card.
>

### The silent failures

These fail without raising an exception. The only defence is a test that asserts the invariant directly,
which is why stage 13 precedes stage 21. Five were identified in v3, covering Stages 4 through 8. Eight
more, extending the same methodology through Step 14, are new in v4 and specified in full in §13.

| # | BUG | SYMPTOM | TEST |
|—|—|—|—|
| 1 | Unadjusted corporate actions | Spurious -50% returns; ranker learns bonus calendars | No adjusted return outside its price band without an action record |
| 2 | Graph leakage | Nothing errors; IC quietly rises | Every graph snapshot built from trailing windows only |
| 3 | Normalisation leakage | Invisible; IC roughly doubles | Cross-sectional z-score per date; assert no full-history statistics |
| 4 | Viterbi at inference | Backtest spectacular, live results noise | Assert forward algorithm only |
| 5 | Pretraining leakage | Every fold silently invalid; the most damaging of the five because it contaminates all folds at once | Assert encoder checkpoint cutoff < fold test start - embargo |
 - 6--12 | \multicolumn3p13.6cmSteps 9 through 14 — graph fusion, regime routing, ranking, sizing, conformal calibration, cost-aware abstention, and explanation generation. *New in v4, in full in §13.*

Plus the two from the transfer document that are not model bugs but cost equal time: date-type mismatch

## Mobile Delivery Architecture *(new in v4)
*

### Positioning within Layer 8

§9 names the mobile stack in one line: *React Native / Expo — one mobile codebase, two
stores*. That choice stands. This section specifies what sits around it.

Layer 8 (Delivery) already separates the API from its clients: FastAPI serves Lite, Pro, and Max tier
endpoint families to whichever client calls them. That separation is what makes mobile additive rather
than disruptive — the web app (Next.js) and the mobile app (React Native / Expo) are two clients of
one API, not two independent systems that happen to share a backend.

> **WHY EXPO, NOT BARE REACT NATIVE**
>
> Expo's managed workflow handles code signing, store submission tooling (EAS Build and EAS Submit), and
> over-the-air updates without hand-configuring Xcode and Android Studio build pipelines directly. For a
> single-developer or small-team capstone shipping to both stores under a deadline, this materially
> reduces the surface area an AI coding agent can get subtly wrong. Bare React Native remains an escape
> hatch if a specific native module Expo does not support becomes necessary.

| Apple App Store | Apple Developer Program enrolment; app review covers functionality, data use, and financial-app scrutiny | LYNX names entry, target, and stop-loss levels. Apple's review guidelines on finance apps require clear disclaimers that content is informational, not investment advice — the disclaimer invariant already in §11.1 satisfies this, but it must be visible pre-download in the App Store listing itself, not only inside the app |
| Google Play Store | Play Console developer account; Data Safety form; financial-services content policy declaration | Play's financial-services policy requires disclosure that the app does not execute trades and does not provide personalised investment advice — consistent with the existing signalling-only positioning, but the declaration is a distinct submission step, separate from the in-app disclaimer |
| Both | Privacy policy URL, required for submission to either store | Must state what user data is collected (auth credentials, tier/subscription status) and confirm no trade-execution capability exists |

> **THE SEBI RA QUESTION, RESTATED FOR APP STORES**
>
> §11.3 already flags "Paid tiers vs SEBI RA" as a risk: consideration in exchange for
> entry/target/stop-loss levels is the textbook form of a recommendation, and SEBI Research Analyst
> registration may apply once payment is involved. Store submission raises the stakes on this unchanged
> risk rather than creating a new one — both Apple and Google require an explicit financial-content
> declaration at submission time, which means this question must be resolved, not just documented, before
> either store submission, not merely before public launch as originally scoped.

### Offline behaviour and update strategy

LYNX produces at most ten published signals per day, generated once nightly (Layer 5--6) and remaining
valid until the next cycle. This shapes what the mobile client needs to handle when offline:

- **Cache the last published signal set locally.** A user opening the app without connectivity
sees the most recent signals with a visible timestamp, not a blank screen or an error state.
- **No offline computation.** PRISM never runs on-device. The mobile app is a pure client of
the FastAPI backend; this keeps the invariant "PRISM is the only decision-maker" (§6.1) intact
regardless of delivery surface.
- **Over-the-air updates via Expo** for JavaScript-layer changes (UI fixes, copy changes)
without a new store submission cycle. Native-layer changes (new permissions, new native modules) still
require full store review.

### Authentication carries over unchanged

§9 already specifies WebAuthn plus Argon2id — passkeys, not stored biometrics. This applies
identically on mobile: platform biometric APIs (Face ID, Android biometric prompt) authenticate locally
against a device-held key, and the passkey standard is what actually reaches the server. No biometric
data is transmitted or stored server-side on any client, web or mobile.

## Extended Invariant-Test Methodology *(new in v4)
*

### The pattern, stated generally

§11.2 names five silent failures — bugs that produce no exception, no error, and often a
*better-looking* result, which is what makes them dangerous. The defence documented there is a
test that asserts the invariant directly, rather than trusting that correct-looking output means correct
logic. That pattern was applied through Stage 8 (pretraining leakage). This section extends it forward
through Stage 16, because a coding agent implementing the ranking, sizing, conformal, and explanation
stages is exactly as capable of producing plausible-but-wrong code as one implementing the encoder was.

The general form, for any new component: identify the one failure mode that would look like success —
runs without error, produces a plausible number — while quietly relying on information or logic the
system is not supposed to have. Write a test against a hand-constructed input where you know the correct
answer independently of the model, and confirm the test fails against a deliberately broken
implementation before trusting that it passes against the real one.

### Extended silent-failure table

| # | STAGE | BUG | SYMPTOM | TEST |
|—|—|—|—|—|
| 6 | Step 9 — Multi-hop graph fusion (R-GAT) | Attention aggregates over a future-dated edge (graph snapshot not properly trailing-windowed at the batch level, even if Stage 5's snapshot itself is correct) | Nothing errors; IC quietly rises, same signature as failure 2 | Feed a synthetic node whose only informative edge is dated after the prediction date; assert the prediction is unchanged from the no-edge case |
| 7 | Step 10 — Regime routing (HMM) | Same failure class as the Viterbi invariant, but at the routing layer specifically: a routed head trained end-to-end could let gradient leak regime information computed with future observations | Backtest spectacular, live results noise — same symptom as failure 4, different injection point | Assert the regime label used to route any training sample at time t is computed using only observations up to t |
| 8 | Step 11 — Multi-horizon ranking | Horizon labels misaligned by an off-by-one, so the 5-day head is silently trained against 4-day or 6-day forward returns | Nothing errors; the model trains, converges, and the loss curve looks normal | Construct a hand-labelled toy sequence with a known price path; assert each horizon head's label matches the manually computed forward return for exactly that horizon |
| 9 | Step 12 — Trade sizing (quantile heads) | Quantile crossing: the tau=0.1 output ends up above the tau=0.5 or tau=0.9 output, producing a stop-loss above the entry price or a target below it | Nothing errors; individual predictions look like normal numbers, only wrong relative to each other | Assert tau=0.1 output ≤ tau=0.5 output ≤ tau=0.9 output on every published signal; reject publication if violated |
| 10 | Step 13 — Conformal calibration | Calibration window itself contains data from inside the test fold (the 250-day window drawn without respecting the fold boundary), inflating apparent confidence | Coverage looks correct in backtest, degrades live — the conformal-specific version of leakage | Assert every date in the calibration window strictly precedes the earliest test-fold date; recompute the bound on a synthetic case with a known analytical quantile and check it matches |
| 11 | Step 13 — Cost-aware abstention | Round-trip cost comparison uses a stale or wrong-side cost figure (long-side cost applied to what is structurally a short, or a cost constant that does not reflect §8.2's date-versioned rates) | Signals publish that should have abstained; net returns look worse than backtest with no obvious cause | Assert the cost function called at publish time matches the position side, and assert its output changes across the STT regime-change dates already documented at Stage 6 |
| 12 | Step 14 — Explanation generation | LLM narration states a fact not present in the SHAP attribution or retrieved evidence passed to it — ungrounded generation slipping past the "extraction only, never generative judgment" invariant (§11.1) that was written for the knowledge graph but applies equally here | Explanation reads fluently and plausibly; nothing flags it as unsupported | Assert every named driver in the generated explanation text appears in the SHAP feature list or the retrieved evidence set passed as input; reject and regenerate or fall back to a templated explanation if not |

> **ON QUANTILE CROSSING SPECIFICALLY**
>
> This is worth flagging as the single most likely of the new failures to actually occur, because
> quantile regression trained with independent pinball losses per quantile has no structural guarantee of
> monotonicity between quantiles unless it is explicitly enforced. An AI coding agent implementing "three
> quantile heads with pinball loss" from a natural-language description will very plausibly produce three
> independently trained heads with no crossing constraint, because the natural-language description does
> not obviously imply one is needed. Test #9 above is not optional.

### Where this leaves the full invariant suite

Combined with §11.2, the full test suite now covers every stage from corporate action adjustment (Stage
4) through explanation generation (Step 14) with a targeted adversarial test — twelve tests in total,
four already passing from the existing build, eight new ones specified above still to be written as
those stages are implemented. Delivery (Layer 8) and self-improvement (Layer 9) are excluded from this
table deliberately: they are operational rather than statistical, and their correctness is better
verified through integration testing than through the hand-constructed-input pattern used here.

## Build Sequencing and Session Discipline

### Backend first, clients second

This subsection records the sequencing decision that governs how §12 and §13 actually get built.

- **Backend first.** Complete and verify Layers 0 through 7 (data through explanation) plus the
Layer 8 API surface, using the extended invariant-test methodology in §13 alongside the existing §11.2
tests, before beginning either client application.
- **Clients second.** Build the Next.js web client and the React Native / Expo mobile client
against the verified, stable API. Both clients consume the same endpoints; neither embeds any decision
logic.
- **Rationale.** A UI defect is visible the moment the screen renders and is cheap to fix at any
point in the app's lifetime, including as a post-launch update. A defect in the ranking, calibration, or
regime-routing logic is frequently invisible — it produces a plausible number, not an error — and
becomes far more expensive to find once client applications, and the review cycles of two app stores,
are built on top of it.

Multiple AI coding tools and agents may be used to write both the backend and the client applications.
For the backend specifically, correctness is verified through the targeted invariant tests in §11.2 and
§13, written per risky component, rather than through code review alone.

### Session discipline

The transfer document identifies scope drift across long AI-assisted sessions as a real cost: the system
was silently rewritten as swing-only, then given a trade-execution module that violated a core
invariant. Two habits prevent recurrence.

- Re-paste this document at the start of every session. It is the canonical architecture; anything
contradicting it is drift.
- Keep an explicit forbidden list. For LYNX: no trade execution; no LLM in the decision path; no
generative facts in the knowledge graph; no learned gating in the regime router; no architecture search;
no pretraining across a fold boundary.
